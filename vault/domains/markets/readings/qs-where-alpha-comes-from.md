# Where Alpha Comes From, and Why It Disappears

Argues every persistent source of return traces to exactly one of three origins: risk premia (you're paid to bear a risk others don't want and eventually get hurt by it), behavioral errors (someone predictably keeps making the same mistake until the trade gets published and arbitraged away), or structural forces (someone is forced to trade suboptimally by rules or liquidity and can't stop). Works through five decay mechanisms, arbitrage, crowding, capacity, the McLean-Pontiff publication effect (average roughly 58% decay after a factor is published), and cost erosion, and concludes edge now survives mainly in harder-to-process data, less-covered names, harder-to-trade horizons, and more complex combinations, not liquid large-cap daily data.
