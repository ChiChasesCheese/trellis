# Trading 101: A Zero-Knowledge Primer

A zero-background primer that establishes the small vocabulary every later document assumes: a strategy as a mechanical, backtestable rule; a signal as the number driving it; Sharpe ratio as return-per-unit-of-risk rather than raw return; max drawdown as the worst peak-to-trough loss; and beating the market as beating a buy-and-hold benchmark on a risk-adjusted basis, which is genuinely hard because the market already prices in the work of everyone else trying to do the same thing. It closes with a first taste of survivorship bias as an example of how a backtest can lie to you convincingly.
