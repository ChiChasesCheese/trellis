# Family 1: Momentum & Trend

Introduces momentum as a behavioral-origin edge: investors under-react to new information, so prices drift toward fair value instead of jumping there, and disposition-effect sellers who take profits too early hand momentum traders the other side of the trade. Distinguishes cross-sectional momentum (rank a basket, buy the strongest, sell the weakest) from time-series/trend-following (each asset judged against its own history, giving it directional market exposure and making it the family that profits in crashes). Also covers dual momentum, residual momentum, 52-week-high anchoring, breakout entries, and cross-asset trend breadth, each with a note on why it works and who is on the losing side.
