# Long/Short, Neutral, Self-Financing: Explained Through FX Value

Uses a real long-short FX-value strategy to teach the vocabulary of relative-value investing from scratch: what going long/short means, why matching long and short dollar amounts creates near-zero market beta, and why a long-short book is roughly self-financing (short-sale proceeds fund the long leg, so uninvested capital keeps earning the risk-free rate). It then explains why this makes Sharpe ratio, not raw return, the right yardstick for such strategies — leverage rescales return and drawdown together but leaves Sharpe unchanged — and why an unglamorous 6.6%-a-year market-neutral strategy can be more valuable in a portfolio than a flashier long-only one.
