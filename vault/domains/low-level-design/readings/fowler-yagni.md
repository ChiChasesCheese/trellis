# Yagni (Martin Fowler, bliki)

The definitive essay on not building for presumptive features — it names the
four real costs of speculative work and draws the crucial line between
skipping features and skipping design quality.

**Extract on read:**
- The four costs: cost of build, cost of delay, cost of carry (complexity taxing
  every later change), cost of repair.
- Yagni applies to presumptive *features*, never to refactoring or clean code —
  "yagni is only viable with cheap-to-change code."
- The interview framing: extensibility comes from seams and small interfaces,
  not from pre-built abstraction layers.
