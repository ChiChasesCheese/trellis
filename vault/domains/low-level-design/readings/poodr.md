# Practical Object-Oriented Design (Sandi Metz)

The best book at treating each OOP pillar as a lever with a cost — every
chapter asks "what does this design choice make cheap to change?" instead of
reciting definitions. Ruby examples, fully language-transferable.

**Extract on read:**
- Ch. 2–3: single responsibility and managing dependencies — encapsulation as
  "depend on things that change less often than you do."
- Ch. 6: the real price of inheritance (the hook-method contract) and when a
  shallow hierarchy is still the right call.
- Ch. 4–5: designing around messages, not classes — polymorphism as trusting
  the receiver.
