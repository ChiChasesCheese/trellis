# Drill: Film study of your own game, counting decisions

Take one full recording of yourself playing — a league game, a long pickup
run, a phone on a tripod. Watch it once with a single written question at the
top of the page, and tally **decisions**, not outcomes. A made contested
pull-up with an open corner shooter is a bad decision that worked; it goes in
the same column as a miss.

**How to run it**
1. Write one question before pressing play. "Where was the low man on every drive I took?" is a question. "Was I good?" is not.
2. Log every possession you touch the ball on: what the defence gave, what you chose, what the better option was.
3. Tally into three columns — correct read, defensible read, wrong read — with the outcome recorded separately.
4. Pull exactly two clips: one rep of the thing you do well under pressure, one rep of the error you repeat most.
5. Turn the error clip into a constraint for your next session, not into a resolution.

**Constraints to state and honor**
- One question per viewing. A second question means a second viewing.
- Outcome is recorded but never used to grade the decision.
- Two clips maximum. A twenty-clip reel gets watched once and changes nothing.
- Count possessions where you did *not* touch the ball too — spacing errors are invisible unless you look for them.

**Grading points (what a strong session hits)**
- The question was answerable from the footage and was actually answered ([[bb-film-study-one-question]]).
- Decision counts and outcome counts are kept apart on the page ([[bb-decisions-not-outcomes]]).
- Shot quality is judged by shooter, situation and score, not by whether it fell ([[bb-shot-quality-vs-shot-making]]).
- The repeated error is named specifically enough to become a constraint ([[bb-constraints-led-approach]]).
- Off-ball possessions are reviewed, not skipped.
