# Drill: 3-on-3 pick-and-roll, defence calls the coverage

Half court, three on three: handler, screener, and one spacer against three
defenders. Before each possession the defence quietly picks a coverage — drop,
ICE, hedge, switch, or blitz — and does not announce it. The offence has one
possession to diagnose which one it is and play the answer. After the
possession, the handler says out loud what he thought it was **before** the
defence reveals it.

**Constraints to state and honor**
- The defence must commit to the called coverage for the whole possession. No improvising a second scheme mid-rep.
- The offence gets one screen. A re-screen is allowed only if the handler names the coverage correctly first.
- The spacer may not stand still: he lifts, drifts or cuts on every rep.
- Score two ways — one point for the bucket, one point for a correct diagnosis. Track them separately.
- Rotate so every player handles, screens and spaces at least four times.

**Grading points (what a strong rep hits)**
- The diagnosis comes from the big's position and hips at the moment of contact, not from what happened afterwards ([[bb-using-ball-screens]]).
- Drop is answered at the level the big is sitting, not with a forced rim attack.
- Against a hedge or blitz, the ball leaves early and the short roll becomes a 4-on-3 launcher.
- ICE is met by rejecting or by using the sideline as the trap it is meant to be.
- The spacer's movement keeps the passing angle alive rather than decorating the corner.
- Diagnosis accuracy improves across the session even when scoring does not.
