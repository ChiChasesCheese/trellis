# Drill: 1-on-1 from a live catch, two dribbles

One passer, one offensive player, one defender. The offence starts with no
ball, cuts to get open, catches on the move, and has **two dribbles** to
produce a shot. Play to five makes, then switch. The constraint is the whole
drill: two dribbles removes the option of solving the possession by dribbling
until something happens, so the advantage has to be created before or at the
catch.

**Constraints to state and honor**
- Two dribbles maximum. A third dribble is a turnover, not a reset.
- The catch must be live — moving, off a cut, with the defender attached. No stationary handoffs at the top.
- Defender plays honest denial; he is allowed to overplay, which makes the backdoor a legal answer.
- Shot clock of five seconds from the catch. Late shots count as misses even if they go in.
- Alternate which foot you land the catch on across sets, and note which one produced more advantage.

**Grading points (what a strong rep hits)**
- The first move happens before the catch — the cut sets up the attack angle.
- You attack the defender's top foot rather than the space beside him.
- Deceleration is used as the move, not as the pause before one ([[bb-change-of-pace-secrets]]).
- The counter is chosen from what the defender did, not decided in advance.
- Overplay is punished by going behind him, not by fighting through ([[bb-princeton-backdoor-trigger]]).
- Both dribbles have a purpose; a wasted first dribble shows up immediately in this format.
