## Q
Horns, Spain and stack are called *sets*, not offenses. What is the difference, and what is a set actually for?

## A
An **offense** is a rule system that governs a whole possession and can repeat indefinitely. A **set** is a starting alignment plus one or two scripted actions whose only job is to *manufacture the specific two-man game you want*, after which you flow back into your offense.

What each shape is hiding:
- **Horns** — two bigs at the elbows, two players in the corners, ball at the top. Both bigs' defenders are pulled out of the paint, and the defense cannot tell which elbow will screen. It is a fork, not a play.
- **Spain** — a normal ball screen with a back screen added on the screener's defender, which puts one defender in charge of two actions at once.
- **Stack** — bodies lined up on top of each other so the defense cannot see who is cutting where until the stack breaks.

The test of a good set is not whether it scores, but whether the action it produces is one your personnel actually wants.

## Q zh
Horns、Spain、stack 被叫做 *set（战术配置）*，而不是 offense。区别在哪？set 到底是干什么用的？

## A zh
**Offense（进攻体系）** 是管住整个回合、可以无限重复的规则系统。**Set** 则是一个起始站位加上一两个写好的动作，唯一任务是*制造出你想要的那个 two-man game*，之后就重新汇入你的体系。

每个形状藏着什么：
- **Horns** —— 两名内线站两侧肘区，两人在底角，球在弧顶。两名内线的防守人都被拉出禁区，而且防守看不出哪一侧会来掩护。它是一个分叉点，不是一个固定战术。
- **Spain** —— 普通的 ball screen 之上，再对掩护者的防守人加一个 back screen（背掩护），让一个防守人同时负责两个动作。
- **Stack** —— 人叠在一起站，防守在队形散开之前看不出谁往哪切。

判断一个 set 好不好，不在于它是否得分，而在于它跑出来的那个动作，是否正是你手上球员真正想要的。
