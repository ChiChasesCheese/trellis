## Q
When reviewing your own film, why must decisions and outcomes be counted in separate columns — and what happens to a player who grades himself by outcomes?

## A
Because **the outcome contains luck and the decision does not.** A pass into a closing gap that a teammate fumbles was a good decision; a contested pull-up with an open corner shooter that drops was a bad one. Over one game the outcome column is mostly noise; the decision column is mostly you.

**What happens if you grade by outcomes:** you learn from whichever behaviour happened to be rewarded. A hot night teaches you to keep taking a shot you should not take; a cold night talks you out of a shot you should. This is the same failure as judging a lineup by a small-sample plus-minus, applied to yourself, and it is worse because you feel it emotionally.

**Practical method:** three columns — *correct read*, *defensible read*, *wrong read* — and record the outcome beside them without letting it colour the grade. Then track the ratio across sessions. Decision quality moves slowly and honestly; percentage moves fast and lies.

## Q zh
复盘自己的录像时，为什么必须把决策和结果记在两列里？用结果给自己打分的球员会怎么样？

## A zh
因为**结果里含有运气，决策里没有。** 一次传进正在合拢的缝隙、队友没接住的球是好决策；一次在底角射手空位时投进的干扰下后仰，是坏决策。在单场里，结果那一列基本是噪声；决策那一列基本是你。

**用结果打分会怎样：** 你会向"碰巧被奖励"的那种行为学习。手感火热的一晚教你继续投一个不该投的球；手感冰凉的一晚劝你放弃一个该投的球。这和用小样本 plus-minus 评判一套阵容是同一种错误，只不过用在了自己身上，而且更糟，因为你会有情绪。

**实操方法：** 三列 —— *正确的 read*、*说得通的 read*、*错误的 read* —— 结果记在旁边，但不让它影响评分。然后跨多次训练追踪这个比例。决策质量移动得慢而诚实；命中率移动得快而且会骗人。
