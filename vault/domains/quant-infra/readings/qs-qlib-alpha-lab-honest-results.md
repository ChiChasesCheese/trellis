# The Factor-Mining Lab: A Full Honest Accounting

A full honest accounting of a large automated factor-mining program across four markets and roughly 400 experiments: a volatility-family signal survives multiple US universes but still fails the deflated-Sharpe gate because the universe is too narrow for the effect size to be distinguishable from luck — a worked illustration of the IC times root-breadth ceiling on single-factor Sharpe. Broader sweeps across price-volume mechanisms produce zero surviving leads even at thousands of names, while a genuinely new data axis, analyst-guidance surprise, comes closest to passing. The piece also shows, with numbers, that stop-losses don't rescue a failing factor and that naively combining correlated diversifiers can make a portfolio's Sharpe worse, not better.
