## Q
You are ninety seconds into an answer and you realise this story does not actually answer the question you were asked. What are your options, and what is the move to avoid?

## A
The move to avoid: **apologising and restarting from scratch.** It burns two minutes, broadcasts that you were not listening, and the replacement story is now told under stress.

Ranked options, best first:

- **Land it and pivot in one sentence.** Finish quickly, then bridge: *"That is the version where I owned the decision — but the sharper example of disagreement is when..."* Reads as thoroughness rather than a miss.
- **Re-aim the same story.** Usually possible and usually best. Keep the setup you have already paid for and change which decision you open up: *"The interesting part was not the migration, it was that the DBA and I disagreed about..."* The interviewer never knows.
- **Ask, if you genuinely cannot tell what was wanted.** Costs five seconds, reads as calibrated. Once a round, not three times.
- **Abandon and switch** — only if truly irrelevant, in one clean clause with no apology: *"Let me give you a better example."*

The underlying skill is noticing at the 20-second mark, which is why the first sentence out of your mouth should be the decision, not the setup.
