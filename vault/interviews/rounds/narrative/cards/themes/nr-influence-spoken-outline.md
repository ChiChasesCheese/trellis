## Q
You are asked the influence-without-authority question and have two minutes. Give the order of the spoken answer, beat by beat, so that it works for whichever real story you choose.

## A
Say it in this order, one or two sentences each:

1. **The stakes, in one line.** What was at risk and for whom: "release candidates were blocked every week by failures in another team's component."
2. **Why authority was not available.** Different org, their own deadline, no shared manager below the director.
3. **Their side, fairly.** What they were optimising for and why your ask looked like a cost to them.
4. **Your move.** The concrete thing that changed their cost or their incentive: a failure table they had not seen, the first fix written by you, a one-component pilot.
5. **What made it stick.** The mechanism — a CI gate, an owner, a shared dashboard — so the change survived without you.
6. **The result and what you would do differently.** A number (blocked runs per week, hours of triage saved) and one honest lesson, for example "I should have gone to their lead before the group meeting."

Keep beat 4 the longest: it is the part the interviewer will drill into, and the part a borrowed story cannot support. Pick the real story first and fit it to the beats; never invent details to fill one.
