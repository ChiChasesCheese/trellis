## Q
"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague." Your instinct is to end with "and I convinced them, and it turned out I was right." Why does that ending score worse than it feels, and what endings score better?

## A
Why it underperforms: the question is not testing whether you win arguments. It is testing whether **a team can survive disagreeing with you.** "I was right and they came around" answers a question nobody asked, and signals that your disagreements only resolve when you get your way.

There is also a structural problem: if the point is that you were right, the interviewer cannot separate good judgment from a lucky outcome, because you gave them only the outcome.

Better endings, ascending:

- **"We ran a cheap test that would settle it."** Converting an opinion fight into an experiment is the most senior move available here.
- **"I was partly wrong, and their objection changed my design."** Costs nothing and is very hard to fake.
- **"They made the call, I disagreed, I executed it properly, and here is how I made sure we'd notice early if it went badly."** Commitment with an instrument attached.
- **"We escalated together, with both positions written down."**

What is actually measured is **what the disagreement cost the relationship.** A good answer makes clear you still worked with that person afterwards.
