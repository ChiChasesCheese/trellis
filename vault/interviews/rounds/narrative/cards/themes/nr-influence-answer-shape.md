## Q
"Tell me about a time you influenced a team you had no authority over." Which elements must the answer contain, and which phrasings quietly signal a junior answer?

## A
**Must contain:**

- **The stakeholder map.** Who owned the thing, who felt the pain, who set priorities. Naming three people shows you understood the system of people, not just the code.
- **Their objection, stated fairly.** "They were two weeks from their own launch and saw my request as scope creep." If you cannot state their side well, the interviewer assumes you did not listen.
- **The move that changed the cost.** The data, the PR you wrote for them, the pilot, the shared metric — something concrete that made yes easier.
- **The mechanism that made it last.** A gate in CI, a shared dashboard, an owner rotation. Influence that needs you in every meeting has not finished.
- **A number and the relationship afterwards.** What improved, and evidence you still work well with them.

**Phrases that read as junior:**

- **"I escalated to my manager and they made it a priority."** That is borrowing authority, the opposite of the question.
- **"I convinced them."** Says nothing about how; sounds like persistence, not judgment.
- **"They eventually realised I was right."** Makes the other team the problem.
- **No cost to you.** A real influence story usually includes something you gave up or did yourself to make it happen.
