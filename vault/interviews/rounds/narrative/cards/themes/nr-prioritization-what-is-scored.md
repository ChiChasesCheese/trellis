## Q
"Tell me about a time you had more work than time." Many candidates answer with how hard they worked to get everything done. Why does that score poorly, and what is the interviewer actually scoring?

## A
**"I worked late and did it all" answers a stamina question nobody asked.** It also hints that you cannot say no, which at senior level is a risk, not a virtue.

What is scored:

- **An explicit criterion.** How you ranked the work: user impact, risk if skipped, reversibility, who is blocked. A rule someone else could reapply beats "I used my judgment".
- **A visible cut.** What you deliberately did *not* do, written down. The cut is the evidence of prioritisation; without one there was no prioritising, only working.
- **Who you told, and when.** The person who wanted the dropped item heard it from you early, with the reason, before they discovered it.
- **The risk you accepted and how you watched it.** "We shipped without the long-run soak test; I added an alert on memory growth and checked it daily for a week."
- **The outcome of the cut.** Did the thing you skipped matter later? Saying "yes, and here is what I changed" is stronger than a story where every bet paid off.

The best answers sound calm: a small number of decisions, each with a reason, rather than a heroic list of everything that got done.
