## Q
You structure an answer perfectly as Situation, Task, Action, Result — and it still scores as unremarkable. What is the structural flaw, and what shape fixes it?

## A
The flaw: **STAR is organised around what happened, and interviewers are scoring how you think.** Situation and Task are pure setup and consume the listener's best attention; "Action" collapses a decision and its execution into one blob, so the thing being graded — the judgment — never gets its own sentence.

The shape that works, the **decision spine**:

1. **Situation, in two sentences.** Only the facts that make the decision hard.
2. **The decision that was yours.** Stated as a decision: *"I had to choose between X and Y, and nobody above me was going to make that call."*
3. **The option you rejected, and why.** The highest-value sentence in the answer and the one almost everybody omits. It is the only proof you saw a tradeoff rather than one obvious path.
4. **Result, with a cost attached.** What improved, and what it cost.
5. **What it changed in you.** A mechanism, not a sentiment. "We now block the deploy on it", not "I learned to communicate more."

Fifteen-second diagnostic on any story: **can you point at one sentence that is the rejected alternative?** If not, you have narrated events, not demonstrated judgment.
