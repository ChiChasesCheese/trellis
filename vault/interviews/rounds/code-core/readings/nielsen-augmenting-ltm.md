# Augmenting Long-term Memory (Michael Nielsen)

The best essay on turning things you have understood into things you still know
months later. Nielsen is specific about what makes a good spaced-repetition
prompt: atomic, unambiguous, and about a rule rather than a fact you will never
use. The section on "the most important thing is to make the cards atomic" is the
direct answer to what to do with a finished assessment — one lesson per card,
stated so it applies to a problem you have not seen.

**Extract on read:**
- Atomicity: one card tests one thing, or you cannot tell which half you forgot
  ([[cc-transfer-playbook-card-the-rule]]).
- Why cards about your own conclusions on a specific artefact fail, and cards
  about transferable rules survive.
- Using the system to hold the shape of a whole subject, not just facts —
  which is what turns a round into a playbook ([[cc-transfer-playbook-find-the-general-leaf]]).
